Lunar Landing Site Navigator

Every landing at the Moon's south pole is a bet on light. We do the maths before you place it.

Scroll to start the universe

13.8 billion years of physics, and the Sun still won't climb two degrees over the place we want to land.

Meet the system. One Moon, its axis tilted a mere 1.5°. One Earth, nodding at it every month.

Fly south.

Below 80° the ground is lit by a Sun that never climbs. Shadows run for kilometres; craters hold night that has lasted billions of years. This relief is NASA's LOLA laser altimetry, lit the way the pole is lit: from 8° above the horizon.

Gold diamonds are candidate landing sites: the nine Artemis III regions and the landers that went before.

Here the Sun doesn't rise. It circles.

Earth plays hide and seek.

Every byte home goes straight to Earth: Artemis III assumes no relay. When Earth hangs a degree or two over the terrain, the signal bounces off the ground on the way out (multipath), and the link fades.

Small moves, big consequences.
